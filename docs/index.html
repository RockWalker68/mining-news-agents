  • Novel geochemical anomaly detection for porphyry copper systems using UMAP–Hypergraph–Kmeans (UHK): central sector of the Kerman cenozoic magmatic Arc, SE Iran Ore Geology Reviews

    Introduces UHK, an unsupervised method combining manifold learning, hypergraphs and clustering, which captured 85% of known porphyry copper deposits within 15% of the study area.

  • Multiple mineral chemistry records the melt-fluid evolution history and rare-metal enrichment of the Murong spodumene pegmatite deposit, SW China Ore Geology Reviews

    Shows how two successive fluid stages from the Murong LCT pegmatite moved boron, lithium, tantalum and niobium into schist and enriched the late melt in rare metals.

  • Review of regional lake sediment geochemistry methods for mineral exploration in glaciated terrain Geochemistry: Exploration, Environment, Analysis

    Review of lake sediment geochemical surveys across glaciated Canada sets out sampling, processing, QA/QC and interpretation protocols for detecting bedrock mineralization and glacial dispersal trains.

  • Extreme light rare earth element enrichment in carbonatite-related rare earth element deposits driven by coupled immiscibility, fractional crystallization, and melt–wall rock metasomatism Ore Geology Reviews

    Maoniuping carbonatite data show REE-mineralized rock reaches LREE/HREE ratios near 310, with calcite recording rising REE and (La/Yb)N from about 3 to 22 during evolution toward ore.

  • Lithospheric step steepness controls magmatic Ni–Cu sulfide potential at craton margins Ore Geology Reviews

    A geodynamic model uses the steepness of the lithospheric step at craton margins to rank magmatic Ni-Cu sulfide potential, with steep steps matching the world's giant deposits.

  • Multiple-stage beryllium mineralization in the Mikengshan volcanic basin, Jiangxi Province, South China: Implications for the volcanic-hosted beryllium metallogenic potential Ore Geology Reviews

    Beryl, phenakite, topaz and bertrandite mineralisation in the Mikengshan volcanic basin records three stages and dates to about 132 Ma, showing Be potential beyond Spor Mountain.
