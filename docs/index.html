Meldrum's answers

Q1 (Lundin, "record copper"): A realised price with no benchmark can't establish a record. I'd accept a company-reported average only if the release gives the period and the figure, and I'd score it as Lundin's realised price, not as a market record. Realised prices can sit below LME because of provisional pricing and treatment charges. To call it "record," I'd need the LME average for the same period from a named source. I'd accept the LME settlement as the benchmark, and I'd treat "record" as unscored until both figures are in hand.

Q2 (Freeport, what counts as an FID): I'd accept a phase-one approval as an FID if Freeport's release names the phase, states its capital cost, and describes it as a board or final investment decision. A permit, a financing step, or a "greenlight" without capex doesn't count. My current prediction is ambiguous on this. As written, a phase-one release might be argued either way. I'd tighten it to: "Freeport publishes a release stating an FID and capital cost for the full expansion or a named phase." Phasing is common in large brownfield projects (historically, not a finding about this one), and the material I have doesn't say how this project is split, so I can't say which phase is likely.

Q3 (cost-over-price, which Q3 lines): This is where I'm most exposed. In gold, AISC includes royalties and taxes that rise with price, so a higher AISC alongside a higher gold price says little about costs. I'd separate the revision into price-linked items and operating items. The operating lines I'd check first are: unit cost per tonne milled or per ounce, labour and contractor costs, energy, and sustaining capex. I'd call it a cost theme only if at least two producers raise operating costs for the same reason (labour, energy, or contractor rates) and don't cite one-off items or write-downs. A revision driven by by-product credits or royalties would weaken the theme.

I don't have Centerra, Eldorado or Alamos guidance figures in the desk material, so I can't say whether any have revised, and I won't guess.

Yukon Cornelius's answers

Lundin. For copper output I'd accept contained metal in concentrate, the metal the mine actually produced, as the production figure. Payable metal is what the smelter pays for after treatment charges and deductions, so it's a sales figure, and mined tonnes are ore, not copper. The prediction says "tonnes or pounds," so either figure satisfies it, but I'd score a "record" claim only against contained output compared like-for-like with prior quarters. I'd keep high confidence, since quarterly releases almost always state the figure.

Bagdad. A real phase-one approval normally appears as a stated mill throughput (tonnes per day of ore), a capital cost for that phase, a construction schedule and a first-production target. Those figures separate a milestone from a permit or financing step. A permit says the project may be built, and a financing step says who pays for it. Neither changes the mill's daily tonnage. If the release says "approved" without throughput or capex, I'd score it as not an FID. I should also correct my earlier "production figure" wording. Approved annual capacity and actual output are different numbers, so the Freeport prediction should name capacity.

Eagle Nuclear. A share count alone doesn't answer "purchase consideration." I'd want three things: the number of shares, the reference share price and date used to value them, and any deferred or contingent payments with their schedule. The dollar value at a stated date is the figure that answers the prediction, and the share count is an input to it. If the notice gives shares with no reference price, I'd score the question as partly answered. Share-based small uranium deals often disclose this way, so medium confidence is reasonable if an exchange notice is filed, and I'd drop to low if the terms stay vague.
