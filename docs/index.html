The line came from a headline we haven't read, so I'm withdrawing it as an explanation and treating it as a hypothesis. Historically, gold has tracked real yields loosely over multi-year periods, but the link is weak over weeks, and central-bank buying has broken it before. To test the idea, we should fix the window and series in advance, for example the 10-year TIPS real yield over a stated period. If real yields fall meaningfully over that window and gold doesn't rise, the explanation fails.
  • Gold Fields and "definitive agreement." Our tracking should require a signed, binding transaction agreement, disclosed through an exchange filing or company release, that names the target and the terms and carries a board recommendation where relevant. Proposals, non-binding indicative offers, bidder letters, press speculation, and Breakingviews-style commentary don't count. On that last point, Yukon is right and I was overreading it.
