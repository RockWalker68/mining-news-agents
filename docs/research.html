A perspective on data science and geochemistry for tomorrow's resources

Geochemistry: Exploration, Environment, Analysis · 2026-11-13 · McLean Trott, Matthew I. Leybourne, Juan Carlos Ordóñez-Calderón et al.

General · General (applies across metals) medium relevance

Setting: Not specific; regional exploration through deposit-scale characterization and mine closure

Where it applies: Lower 48 and Alaska alike. These are methods, not a geology, so they fit any district with a good legacy geochemical database, such as the sediment and soil surveys common in both regions. Background: many older Lower 48 and Alaska exploration datasets are sitting unused, so this approach has real practical value.

For explorers: An explorer with a large soil, stream-sediment, or rock-geochemistry dataset could use multivariate and machine-learning workflows to rank anomalies and find pathfinder combinations. The paper also argues for planning geochemical data for reclamation and closure from the start.

A rare type of uranium mineralization in the Husab uranium deposit, Namibia: glimmerite-type uranium mineralization

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-10 · Zhou Haoyu, Guanglai Li, Huaifeng Zhang et al.

Alaskite-type uranium; glimmerite-type uranium veins · Uranium (U3O8), with fluorine and rare earths in apatite low relevance

Setting: Namibian Damara orogen granitic intrusions, Neoproterozoic to Cambrian age (background)

Where it applies: Little direct bearing on the Lower 48 or Alaska. Alaskite-type uranium is rare in both regions, and I know of no glimmerite-type mineralization there.

For explorers: Lower 48 and Alaska explorers are unlikely to change their programs because of this paper. The one transferable idea is that biotite and apatite chemistry can help fingerprint magmatic uranium enrichment in peraluminous granites.

Zircon–apatite fingerprints of porphyry Cu(Au) fertility in Georgetown granitoids, NE Queensland

Journal of Geochemical Exploration · 2026-11-07 · Jonghyun Lee, Ioan V. Sanislav, Helen A. McCoy-West et al.

Porphyry Cu(Au) · Copper, gold high relevance

Setting: Permian and Carboniferous granitoids, Georgetown region, NE Queensland; Tasman/ Thomson-type orogenic belt (background)

Where it applies: Lower 48 Laramide and Cordilleran porphyry belts in Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, and Montana are the best match, since the method targets oxidized, hydrous arc magmas. Alaska's Alaska Range and Aleutian-related porphyry systems also fit the method, though the match is less direct.

For explorers: Explorers working in Laramide or Alaska arc granites could collect zircon and apatite from intrusions and rank them by the Eu/Eu* and ZCFI thresholds before committing to drilling. This is a cheap way to prioritize intrusions when whole-rock data are altered.

Supergene Enrichment of Rare Earth Elements in Weathered Carbonatite Systems: Insights from the Sokli Deposit, Northeastern Finland

Economic Geology · 2026-11-06 · Martin Yan Hei Li, Martin Smith, Sam Broom-Fendley et al.

Carbonatite-hosted REE (weathered carbonatite / phoscorite P-Nb-REE) · Rare earth elements, niobium, phosphate medium relevance

Setting: Paleoproterozoic carbonatite-phoscorite complex in northeastern Finland with a deep, karstic weathering profile

Where it applies: Lower 48 carbonatite REE systems, especially Bear Lodge in Wyoming and Mountain Pass in California. Carbonatite complexes in Missouri and Colorado are also relevant. Alaska has little bearing. Background: weathered carbonatite caps are a known enrichment process in these districts.

For explorers: Explorers on carbonatites in the Lower 48 should map weathering profiles and track which REE minerals survive or dissolve at each stage, rather than sampling fresh rock only. Drilling tests should also cover the phosphatic crust and the karst collapse zones where the enrichment accumulates.

Pyrite trace-element evolution and constraints on lithium sources in the Pingqiao hydrothermal fluorite-cookeite deposit, SW China

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-06 · Zhenzhong Xiang, Lin Ye, Shiyu Liu et al.

Hydrothermal fluorite-cookeite lithium deposit (low-temperature hydrothermal, with pyrite trace-element vectoring) · Lithium, fluorite, with pyrite trace elements (Au, As, Sb, Ni, Co, Se) low relevance

Setting: Mesozoic low-temperature hydrothermal system at the margin of the Youjiang and Sichuan-Yunnan-Guizhou provinces, SW China

Where it applies: Little direct bearing on the Lower 48 or Alaska. The Carlin-type comparison is useful background for Nevada, since it shows that absent Au-As-Sb in pyrite is a meaningful negative signal.

For explorers: Nevada Carlin-trend explorers can use the paper's pyrite-generation approach, separating sedimentary from hydrothermal pyrite, and treat missing Au-As-Sb in pyrite as evidence against ore-stage gold. The lithium part does not transfer.

General · General (magnesium and sulfate in hydrothermal fluids) low relevance

Setting: Laboratory physical chemistry of hydrothermal and planetary-ocean brines

Where it applies: Little bearing on Lower 48 or Alaska exploration. It may help modelling of hot sulfate-bearing ore fluids in porphyry and epithermal systems, and the paper says extrapolation to natural fluids should be cautious.

Unraveling the hydrothermal evolution and exploration vectors of IOA system in the Xiangshannan-Dadiantang deposit, Ningwu District: Insights from SWIR spectroscopy and mineral chemistry

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-04 · Wencheng Zhang, Fangyue Wang, Taofa Zhou et al.

Iron oxide-apatite (IOA) magnetite deposit with chlorite alteration vectoring · Iron (magnetite), with apatite, and trace Mg, Ca, Zn in magnetite medium relevance

Setting: Ningwu volcanic basin, eastern China; Mesozoic intrusion-related iron ore, with alteration of volcanic and intrusive host rock

Where it applies: Lower 48 IOA districts in the Pea Ridge and Pilot Knob area of Missouri, and the Adirondacks in New York, are the best match. Background: SWIR (portable spectrometer) alteration mapping is routine in both the Lower 48 and Alaska, but the Alaska match for IOA is weak.

For explorers: Explorers in Missouri or the Adirondacks could collect SWIR spectra on chlorite-bearing alteration and test whether the Mg-OH shift and IC values track high-TFe zones. Chlorite chemistry from drill core would add a second check.

Sandstone-hosted uranium mineralization in the Lower Cretaceous aeolian red beds: A case study from the Jingchuan deposit in the southwestern Ordos Basin, China

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-03 · Yin Chen, Michel Cuney, Julien Mercadier et al.

Sandstone-hosted uranium (roll-front style, red-bed hosted) · Uranium, with iron sulfides, titanium oxides, and phosphates medium relevance

Setting: Lower Cretaceous aeolian red-bed sandstones of the Ordos Basin, northern China; hydrocarbon-associated reducing systems

Where it applies: Lower 48 sandstone-hosted uranium belts are the main match: the Wyoming Powder River and Wind River basins, the Colorado Plateau, and the Texas Gulf Coast. Background: roll-front deposits in these basins also depend on redox fronts and hydrocarbon-driven reduction. Alaska has little bearing.

For explorers: Lower 48 roll-front explorers could sample for Ti-oxide, clay, and sulfide associations and run sulfur isotopes to find reduction-related signals. These tests would help locate the redox front and pick out where uranium fixed.

Y-shaped faults and their control on orogenic gold mineralization: Field survey and particle flow code (PFC) numerical simulation on the Dongkeng deposit in South China

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-03 · JiaJian LU, Yi Zheng, Xi Chen et al.

Orogenic gold (brittle-ductile shear-zone hosted) · Gold medium relevance

Setting: Brittle-ductile shear zone with NW-trending structures and a subordinate fault to the regional NE-trending Wuchuan-Sihui fault; Cathaysia Block, South China

Where it applies: Alaska's orogenic gold belts, including the Juneau gold belt and the Tintina-related belt of interior Alaska, are a good match, since the structural controls are similar. Lower 48 orogenic gold districts such as the Mother Lode in California and the Carolina slate belt fit the same logic.

For explorers: Alaska and Lower 48 explorers could map subordinate and splay faults in addition to the main shear, and target dilation zones where stress alternates. The paper supports drilling the splay and jog structures before the main fault plane.

Lithium cycling in the source-to-sink system of a typical salt lake in the Western Kunlun: multi-isotopic constraints and end-member quantification

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-01 · Yanji Li, Miao Weiliang, Maoyong He et al.

Salt-lake (brine) lithium, with pegmatite lithium as the source · Lithium, boron, strontium medium relevance

Setting: Quaternary closed salt-lake basins on the Qinghai-Xizang Plateau, Western Kunlun, China

Where it applies: Lower 48 brine lithium in Clayton Valley, Nevada, and the Smackover Formation brines of Arkansas and Texas, are the closest matches. The isotope tracers (δ7Li, δ11B, 87Sr/86Sr) apply directly. Alaska has little bearing.

For explorers: Brine explorers in Nevada or Arkansas could use Li, B, and Sr isotopes to separate deep-fluid inputs from surface recharge before drilling. Sampling springs and the terminal basin for end-member fingerprints would help rank brine sources.
