Meldrum's answers

Q1: What counts as a drawdown, and what would silence mean?

A drawdown is a disclosed draw on the US$150M Bilboes facility: a stated amount taken, a debt balance that rises against that facility, or a financing inflow in the cash flow statement tied to it. Availability or commitment is not a drawdown. Holding an undrawn facility is a liquidity buffer, not a sign of stress.

Silence is weaker evidence than it sounds. If the Q3 release is out by 30 November, the MD&A liquidity section and the debt note should say whether the facility has been used, because a listed miner is normally expected to disclose material borrowing. So I would test the full filing, not the headline, and check three places: the liquidity discussion, the financing line in the cash flow statement, and the debt note. If none of them mentions the facility at all, that is informative, though it could also mean the facility is simply not material yet. I would not read it as a financing crisis.

The prediction should be scored on what the filing says as of its publication date. A drawdown after that date would not count, and I should say so in the note. Read together with the guidance cut, a drawdown would matter more than the facility's existence, because it would suggest cash generation is not covering the gap. I still hold this at low confidence, and I would move it only on the filing's wording.

Q2: Which funding mix matters for Endeavour's US$1.2bn Ivory Coast project?

The project is neutral if most of the capex is funded by non-recourse project debt, a partner's equity, or pre-payments tied to offtake or streaming. It starts to matter for balance-sheet risk if it is funded mainly from corporate debt or cash on hand while sustaining capex, dividends or buybacks continue, or if equity issuance is needed.

The threshold that matters is the ratio of the project's capex to the group's free cash flow and liquidity headroom, and I do not have Endeavour's financials in this material, so I cannot put a number on it. Historically, a mid-tier miner that funds a multi-year build from its own balance sheet is most exposed to a cost overrun or a gold price drop during construction. That is background, not a finding about Endeavour.

Until the funding structure is disclosed, I keep the project as a neutral capital commitment with no supply effect before 2028.
