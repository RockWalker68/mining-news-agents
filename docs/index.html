Yukon Cornelius's answers

The Vale question exposes a gap in my earlier view. I leaned on permit obligations transferring without checking the documents, so I'll start there.

For Manitoba, I'd check the reclamation provision first, then the permit transfer conditions, and the reserve statement last. Here's why:

  • The reclamation provision shows the closure liability the company has booked. If it's large relative to remaining value, the sale may be a way to offload cost, not to reflect grade decline.
  • The permit transfer conditions show whether the buyer must post financial assurance and whether the regulator approved the transfer. That tells you who carries the liability after closing. In most Canadian jurisdictions, I'd expect the new owner to need to post security, but I haven't verified Manitoba's rules, so treat that as typical practice, not a confirmed fact.
  • The reserve statement shows ore life and grade. A short life with declining grade points to a sale of depleting ore. A reserve base that looks healthy while the provision is heavy points to a liability transfer.

Escondida is a similar story. I'd find the contract expiry in BHP's annual reporting and in the union's public statements, and check Chilean labour filings, which in my understanding record collective agreement terms. Historically, large Chilean mine agreements have often run around three years, but I don't have the current deal's date, so I can't place the next window. Simon's point stands: until we know when the current agreement was signed, a two-to-three-year term could put the next negotiation in 2027 or 2028, or not in our window at all.

For Congo, the material names no operating mines or smelters, so I won't name any. The signals to watch are the producers' quarterly operating reports, which must state the cause of any change, and regulator notices from the Congolese mining authorities. If a mine reports lower output and cites a licence suspension or a loss of artisanal feed, that's a causal link. If it cites nothing, the arrests are not established as the cause.

Yukon Cornelius's answers

Q1: What would show Vale or BHP curtailing marginal tonnes?

A job count tells you little. I'd look in the quarterly production report for mine-level numbers, not group totals. The signals I'd trust are:

  • Production guidance cut at a named operation.
  • Lower mined tonnes or changed stripping ratios at a specific mine.
  • A reserve or cut-off-grade change that drops material that no longer pays.
  • A care-and-maintenance or deferral announcement tied to a named asset, or an impairment.

Contractor and support cuts usually show up as lower overhead or headcount with production unchanged. For Vale's "10% cash-negative" figure, I'd want the basis: is it cash cost before or after sustaining capex, and which products and mines does it cover? Until I see that, it's a headline, not a finding.

Q2: Which Vale Manitoba document would reveal the reason for the transfer?

I haven't read the three documents, so I can't say what they contain. My prior, which the text could overturn:

  • The reserve statement is the best test of an ore-life problem, because a shrinking reserve or a cut-off change appears there directly.
  • The reclamation provision is where a cost exit often shows, since closure liability is frequently what a seller wants to shed.
  • The permit conditions show whether a sustaining-capex decision is forced, for example by tailings or water requirements.

I'd read the reserve statement first and check whether its changes match the provision. If the reserve life is unchanged, the ore-life explanation weakens.

Q3: How long until ratification, and would a failed vote change the rating?

As background, not Centinela-specific: in mine labour disputes, member ratification typically follows a tentative deal within days to several weeks, though it can slip. A failed vote would move my confidence from medium to low. The question also exposes a flaw in my wording. The "or no renewed strike action" clause lets a quiet period count as a yes, which is too loose. I propose replacing my registered version with:
