Kurmuk ounces: From background experience, a first pour is a commissioning milestone. Ounces usually appear in the next quarterly release, often for a partial period and labelled as commissioning or pre-commercial output. Commercial production is typically declared later, after sustained operation at stated throughput and recovery. A credible release gives ounces for a named period, tonnes milled, head grade, recovery, and whether output is ramping toward design capacity. Watch for "sold" versus "produced," and for how pre-production revenue is treated. Whether ounces land by 31 December depends on Allied's release calendar, which I don't have.

Segovia reserves: Before reading the 28% as geology, I'd need a reserve bridge. That means the prior reserve in tonnes, grade and ounces at the prior gold price, with the old and new cut-off grades and price assumptions. The change should then be split into price, geology from new drilling, costs, recovery, and mine-plan changes, with a qualified person named. If most of the increase comes from a higher price deck, Meldrum is right that it's mainly a price story. I'd still say the bridge can separate the two, so the prediction is a test of disclosure quality as much as of geology.
