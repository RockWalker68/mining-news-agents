Q1, on CMOC: I would drop the "net profit above Q3 2025" wording as written and rewrite it. The call is only useful if the release reports the three months to 30 September 2026, labels that period clearly, restates the Q3 2025 comparative on the same basis, and states attributable net profit. If the document turns out to be a nine-month, half-year, or preliminary profit alert, the Q3 claim can't be scored, and I would void the prediction rather than force it. The tightened wording would be: "CMOC's third-quarter 2026 report, covering the three months to 30 September 2026, shows attributable net profit above the comparable 2025 three-month figure." The 31 October deadline is also tight. Historically, Chinese-listed companies often publish Q3 reports in late October, but that is background practice, not a confirmed date, so a slip would fail the call on timing.

Q2, on the disclosure that matters most: a Copper World FID with capex. I underweighted this. I treated the approval as a limited supply event because the tonnes arrive after 2027 either way. An FID turns "after 2027" from a range into a committed schedule and financing plan, and that is the core of any 2027–2030 timing view. A Red Chris capex figure without a design rate tells us about spending, not tonnes, so it moves the cost picture more than the supply timeline. Red Chris matters more for the back end of the decade, once its design rate is known.

Q3, on diesel: it needs two series. The first is a price: a wholesale diesel benchmark for the mine's region. The EIA publishes weekly US retail diesel prices for free, while Argus and S&P Global Platts publish wholesale benchmarks, usually on subscription. The second is consumption: fuel cost per tonne moved, derived from litres per tonne and the diesel price, which companies disclose in quarterly cost breakdowns. The measurable driver is fuel's share of unit cost, expressed as a change in cash cost per tonne of copper. Until the headline gives a region, a price and a period, I would keep it as a direction only.

Yukon Cornelius's answers

On Red Chris: "Up to US$4.8B" becomes usable only when the release states its basis. I'd check four things, in this order:

  • Study type. Is it a feasibility study with declared reserves, a pre-feasibility study, or a scoping study built on inferred resources? This is usually stated in the first paragraph of the release and in the NI 43-101 technical report. Typically, scoping-study capex carries accuracy in the tens of percent, so it can't be treated like a feasibility number.
  • Design rate. Throughput in tonnes per day, with head grade and recovery.
  • Capex scope. Initial versus sustaining, whether contingency is included, and the currency and base year.
  • Ownership. Is the figure at 100% or Newmont's attributable share?

"Up to" reads to me as a ceiling or a contingency-inclusive upper case, not a point estimate. I had put the design rate first. The study-type label is the faster test, so I'd check it first.

On Copper World: Dr Simon is right that the three predictions overlap. My working definitions:

  • Approval: a permit, regulatory, or board sign-off. It says little about money.
  • FID: the board commits capital, financing is committed, and the release says "final investment decision" or clear equivalent. This is the event that counts.
  • Construction underway: mobilization, groundbreaking, or first major contract, reported separately.

The capex prediction should score the stated total. The FID prediction should score only explicit FID language. The construction line should be dropped or made conditional on FID. An FID release will normally carry the capex, so the three should be tracked as one event with parts. I'm less sure than I was that this is an FID, since trade-press "approval" could mean either. Confidence on the FID line stays medium only until the release wording is read. The 2025-10-21 date on the older line also needs checking against the original source.

On Gunnison: I don't have the Gunnison entry in the material I've been given, so I can't say which confidence was intended. The published line should match the reasoning in the text. If the evidence is thin, the answer is low. Fix the line before the 26 October debut and note the correction in the entry.
