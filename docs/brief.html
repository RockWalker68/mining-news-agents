Dr I Simon's takeaways

Industry health

  • Labour is the binding constraint. The Highland Valley strike, now in its fourth week, keeps copper curtailed, and even a settlement would take weeks to restore full mill and crew operations, so the supply hit outlasts the dispute. Canadian mining wage growth near the bottom points to the same shortage of people.
  • The junior market is active but thin. Drilling and staking are broad, placements are small (Latin Explore's US$2.3M), and consolidation is picking up (VR to Athos, Osiris buying Miracle). That is a tight market, not a boom, and staking should not be read as discovery.

Regional trends

  • North America: Permits and financing are moving, but slowly. Nova's final antimony permits clear a major hurdle, Lion Copper's Bear report moves Nevada copper toward a study, Lithium Americas is rebuilding Thacker Pass financing, and Ottawa and Queen's Park are funding domestic supply (Fireweed's C$14M, NOHFC's C$2.63M). BLM staking, about 2,800 claims mostly in Nevada and Arizona, shows explorers still holding ground.
  • Latin America: Peru's copper growth case strengthened with Pecoy's 727 m at 0.30% copper, and Chile's private-role decision is still pending. Argentina's investment agenda and Brazil's small lithium option are incremental.
  • Middle East, Africa and Asia-Pacific: The Gulf is the largest new risk for mining costs, since tankers were hit in Hormuz and a drone raised alarm at an Iraqi gas field, which lifts diesel, freight, insurance and sulphur costs. Houthi advances toward Aden add Red Sea risk to the same routes. Tanzanian gold contracting continues (Perenti's Geita contract, Nyanzaga commissioning).

Technology & demand

  • Nickel, cobalt and rare earths lose ground at the margin. LFP grid storage (Samsung SDI and Jupiter Power) takes share from nickel and cobalt, and iron and phosphate gain more than lithium per kilowatt-hour. The cobalt-free cathode is lab-stage and should be treated as a signal, while Vespa's rare-earth-free motor trims magnet demand modestly.
  • Copper's AI case is weakening in sentiment, with the chip-export block and the Asian tech sell-off feeding doubts, but no demand data supports a change yet. Nuclear fuel is the firmer AI-linked signal: Centrus's high-assay enriched-uranium contract with Oklo shows advanced reactors lining up fuel, though no volumes are given.

On the horizon

  • Highland Valley: a settlement would start the restart clock, which means copper supply recovers only in the months after a deal. Until then, monthly copper numbers will look weak.
  • US-China summit outcomes (the Trump Asia trip, Quad talks, China visit): the outcome sets the timing of Chinese export licences for rare earths, antimony and tungsten, so it matters more than the sentiment it produces.
  • Nvidia's earnings and the US deep-sea lease sale, due in November with objections from Guam: the first will set the tone for copper's AI narrative, and the second tests US seabed policy.
