Dr I Simon's takeaways

Industry health

  • Concentrate is tight. Negative treatment charges are deepening, smelters are being paid to take feed, and Chile's biggest quarterly economic drop since 2020 shows the mining slump reaching the national accounts.
  • Juniors are drilling again, but the 38% rise in gold exploration budgets comes from a promotional source. Drill releases are running about two to one gold over copper, so gold is pulling the rigs while new copper moves slowly. Freeport's notes fund the balance sheet, and Grasberg's recovery will be judged by block cave and mill rates, not by the bond issue.

Regional trends

  • North America: The Hawaii federal ruling against the Pacific seabed plan is a legal signal, not a tonnage story. It comes from the desk's review and is not in my wire reading, so treat its details as provisional. Ottawa's up-to-C$14M offer for the Yukon tungsten project and its backing for Mactung are policy support, and tonnes are years away. Nevada exploration notices are steady.
  • Latin America: Colombia is becoming a copper district on the back of AngloGold Ashanti's roughly US$2.5bn plan and Collective's US$100M for 2027, and both depend on permits. Chile's output weakness is now a macro story, and Brazil's objection to the EU review of Anglo's nickel sale to MMG adds a trade-policy dispute to the nickel picture.
  • Middle East and Asia: The Gulf is an active conflict, with the Bahrain strike and the Hormuz vessel attack confirmed by wire reports. The Kuwait and Qeshm reports remain unconfirmed. Indonesia's state-share push has no decree yet, and Mongolia's leadership change adds political risk to Oyu Tolgoi.

Technology & demand

  • Nickel and cobalt are losing ground to LFP and sodium-ion grid storage, and lithium faces a long-run sodium-ion threat even though grid storage still adds lithium demand today. The timing is low confidence and the direction is medium confidence. Rare earths and tungsten gain from the Aclara NdPr step and the Western tungsten policy support.
  • Copper's grid case holds, but I'm downgrading the claim that AI data-centre demand pulls copper directly. The Nvidia chip-shipment halt to China weakens that link, while transformers and grid upgrades still run on domestic and allied demand.

On the horizon

  • The US deep-sea lease sale later this month is the next test of the Hawaii ruling. If the sale proceeds anyway, the legal challenge becomes the story and the court's reasoning is what other federal mineral leasing will face.
  • Diesel and sulphur are the cost signals to watch over the next one to three months. Diesel reaches North American haul fleets and reagent freight first, and sulphur reprices SX-EW and HPAL acid on contract cycles.
  • The Trump trip to China, the trilateral with Xi and Putin, and APEC in Shenzhen will show whether China issues multi-year export licences for antimony, tungsten and rare earths. Those licences would restore flow but would not change copper's tightness at the smelter, grid, and permit steps.
