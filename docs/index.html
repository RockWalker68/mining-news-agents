Yukon Cornelius's answers

Punitaqui and the other Chilean restarts: I can't speak to Punitaqui's specifics from this batch. As a general pattern, restarting a shuttered mine typically takes 12 to 24 months from commitment to first concentrate, and longer if it needs dewatering, plant refurbishment, permit amendments or a new resource definition. A partnership announcement is often an MOU or framework. A definitive agreement should state who funds capex and how much, the equity and operatorship split, a schedule with milestones, permit status, and offtake terms. Without those, I'd call it intent, not a restart.

Codelco and Normet: I'd accept disclosures that name a specific mine, state a contracted or delivered fleet count, and report operating data against a baseline, such as availability, tonnes per shift, cycle times, unit cost per tonne, or a safety rate change. A press release announcing a partnership doesn't count. I haven't seen the exact text of Simon's strictest wording in this batch, so I can't confirm it matches my understanding. My view is that the strict version is the right standard, because it requires a named site and operating evidence rather than a corporate statement.

BHP: an automation-driven cut usually names the system, such as the number of autonomous units, the sites involved and rollout dates, and the productivity gain it's meant to deliver. The roles affected are typically operator roles on that equipment. A cost-driven cut cites a savings target with no link to a technology program. I haven't seen a BHP statement in our material, so this is what I'd look for, not what I've checked.

Would it change the Pilbara view? Only modestly. Autonomous haulage has historically been well established there, so much of its cost effect is already in the base. A cost-driven cut would matter more, because it could signal management expects weaker prices or tighter margins. That is a price question for Meldrum to weigh, not a geology one.

Background, not from this study: after the DISCOL experiment in the Peru Basin in the late 1980s, sediment fauna in the disturbed area were still depressed decades later.
