In situ trace elements and U–Pb chronology of cassiterite reveal a long-lived Taoxiba large Sn system in SE China

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-01 · Jinhua Qin, Yan Wang, Liu Shanbao et al.

Intrusive-volcanic-related Sn system (greisen, altered volcanic rock and mica-quartz veins) · Tin (cassiterite); associated Nb, Ta, Sc, W-type trace elements in cassiterite low relevance

Setting: Subvolcanic intrusive-volcanic complex, SE China; ages come from cassiterite U-Pb, and the abstract doesn't state them.

Where it applies: Limited. Alaska's Seward Peninsula has granite-related tin-tungsten greisen and placer systems, such as the Lost River area, so the cassiterite-chemistry approach could be tested there. I see little direct bearing on Lower 48 tin, which is mostly historic and minor.

For explorers: In Alaska's Seward Peninsula, an explorer could use cassiterite trace elements and dating to separate greisen-stage from vein-stage tin and map which events carry economic tin. This is a lab-heavy method and not a field-day change.

Geochemistry and platinum-group mineralogy of chromitites and the limonite zone from the Santa Cruz Ni-laterite deposit, Acoje, Philippines

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-01 · Matías García-Tudela, Thomas Aiglsperger, Diego Domínguez-Carretero et al.

Ophiolite-hosted chromitite and PGE mineralization, with Ni-laterite overprint · Chromite; platinum-group elements (Pt, Pd, Ir, Os, Ru, Rh); nickel low relevance

Setting: Upper mantle section of the Acoje Ophiolite, Philippines; boninite-like melts in a fore-arc setting at subduction initiation; age not given in the abstract.

Where it applies: Modest. Alaska has ultramafic and zoned Alaskan-type complexes with chromite and PGE (for example Duke Island and Goodnews Bay-type settings), so the PGM and "two chromitite generations" logic could help there. Lower 48 has few comparable settings, and Ni-laterites are essentially absent.

For explorers: In Alaska's ultramafic belts, an explorer could sample chromitite and dunite separately and run PGM mineralogy to tell early IPGE-rich from later PPGE-rich phases before deciding which zone to drill. Ni-laterite exploration itself is not relevant here.

Setting: Jiangnan Orogenic Belt, South China; Lengjiaxi metasedimentary Group sourcing the Mufushan granite batholith; ages not given.

General, concealed mineralization under transported cover (applies across porphyry, epithermal and other types) · General; the methods target mobile ore-forming elements such as Au, Cu, Ag and others not specified in the abstract medium relevance

Setting: Arid, thickly covered terrain in NW China (Gobi Desert); transported overburden, extreme aridity; no specific ages or host rocks.

Where it applies: Real relevance for Lower 48 covered terranes in the Basin and Range, especially Nevada and Arizona, where alluvial cover hides bedrock. Soil-gas, mobile-metal-ion and plant-based methods are already used there. Alaska's permafrost and vegetation make these methods less useful, so the fit is mainly Lower 48.

For explorers: In Nevada or Arizona valley-fill areas, an explorer could pick deep-penetrating soil or gas methods suited to the depth and aridity of cover, and set survey density by scale (district, then deposit) rather than applying one grid everywhere.

Ore Geology Reviews · 2026-11-01 · Cheng‐Biao Leng, Dazhao Wang, Yanjun Wang

Porphyry molybdenum · Molybdenum; sulfur (isotopes) medium relevance

Setting: Giant porphyry Mo deposit, NE China; age not given in the abstract.

Where it applies: Direct relevance to Lower 48 porphyry Mo, such as the Climax and Henderson systems in Colorado and related Urad and Mount Emmons-type deposits, because the isotopic vectors are about how molybdenite precipitates. Alaska has few porphyry Mo systems, so the fit there is weak.

For explorers: On Colorado-type porphyry Mo projects, an explorer could compare Mo and S isotopes between the hydrothermal core and the grade centre to map where fluid was most sulfur-limited. The method is still research-grade, so treat it as a guide to sampling, not a drilling rule.

Setting: Lower Carboniferous volcano-sedimentary rocks, West Junggar, NW China; pre-ore chlorite-prehnite-epidote background.

Where it applies: Strong fit for Alaska's Juneau gold belt in southeast Alaska (Kensington and Treadwell-type systems), where orogenic gold is hosted in mafic and volcano-sedimentary rocks with white mica and carbonate alteration. For explorers: In the Juneau belt or Mother Lode-type districts, an explorer could run SWIR on core and surface samples and use white-mica crystallinity (Dep2200/Dep1900) of about 2.5 or higher as a high-grade vector, with carbonate and Pos2200 readings as secondary checks.

General · General (applies across metals) medium relevance

Setting: Not specific; regional exploration through deposit-scale characterization and mine closure

Where it applies: Lower 48 and Alaska alike. These are methods, not a geology, so they fit any district with a good legacy geochemical database, such as the sediment and soil surveys common in both regions. Background: many older Lower 48 and Alaska exploration datasets are sitting unused, so this approach has real practical value.

For explorers: An explorer with a large soil, stream-sediment, or rock-geochemistry dataset could use multivariate and machine-learning workflows to rank anomalies and find pathfinder combinations. The paper also argues for planning geochemical data for reclamation and closure from the start.
