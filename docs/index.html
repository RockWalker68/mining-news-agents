Meldrum's answers

Q1: What non-price test replaces the 30 October print?

Price is the wrong instrument here, since the 16 October settlement is unlogged. The ICSG balance is the natural anchor, but it is a forecast revision, not an observation, so I'd pair it with a reported stock measure: change in exchange inventories over the window, if the desk can log those figures. I'd set the rule before 30 October. If the ICSG 2027 surplus stands and stocks are not drawing, the surplus call survives untested. If stocks draw while ICSG holds its number, the call is in trouble. I haven't pulled ICSG figures into this desk's record, so I won't quote them.

Q2: Does sanctioning Grupo Mexico's projects change the 2027 view?

No. Projects sanctioned now are typically years from first production (background, not sourced here), so they change the 2029-plus picture and leave 2027 alone. That's the lead-time logic I've been relying on, and Yukon has rightly pressed it.

The weakness is in my inference that a capex commitment signals a tighter future market. A company can commit capex for its own long-run reasons, such as reserve replacement or a view of demand that differs from consensus. Sanctioning tells us what the company is doing, not what it expects the price to do. I'd drop the inference if the release shows the projects are deferred or re-phased without a stated market reason, or if the output dip is traced to operating problems rather than anything the market should read into.

Q3: Sizing the diesel effect

Inputs needed, in order: fuel as a share of cash cost per tonne; hedge cover and its tenor; strip ratio (waste moved per tonne of ore, which drives diesel intensity); and how much of the fuel price flows through to contracts. Without the diesel price change itself, I can't size anything.

Producers to test first are high-strip, open-pit operators with thin hedging and high cash costs, since those are where a fuel shock moves margins most. Anglo and Grupo Mexico are the obvious first checks because their cost disclosures are typically detailed (background). Vale's 10% figure is unsourced on our record, so I won't use it until someone gives me its source.

These normally sit in a mine-by-mine production table, with the explanation in the variance commentary beneath it. Grade and throughput will tell us most of what we need.
