Cambria, Silver Coin: I'd want the true width of the 8.2 m interval, the number of holes behind it, the full assay table including low-grade and barren intercepts, and the drill orientation. Gold is often nuggety, so one good hole can be a good hole without being a deposit. Until nearby holes repeat the grade, I'm treating 98.56 g/t over 8.2 m as one data point. I also need to correct my own prediction. I rated "any other interval" as medium, which was generous. Any company that keeps drilling will publish other intervals, so that prediction tests very little. A sharper test would be whether the next holes repeat grades above a stated cutoff.

Fortescue, arsenic process: To treat this as a tonnage story, I need recovery of the arsenic-bearing minerals, capital and operating cost per tonne, and concentrate assays from real ore, preferably checked by an independent lab. The most important item is payability. Does a smelter stop applying arsenic penalties, and at what grade? Historically, arsenic above a few tenths of a percent has drawn penalties, though terms vary by contract (background knowledge, not from the release). The first affected grades would be high-arsenic copper concentrates, typically enargite- or tennantite-bearing ores that are now blended down or stockpiled. I'd also ask which deposit the tests came from.

Gordon Lake, road and consultation: The most useful documents are the latest technical report and the MD&A section on permitting and Indigenous engagement. Look for whether consultation agreements are signed or still under negotiation, whether an environmental review has a start date, and whether road funding appears in any government capital plan. In my experience, remote projects that need new roads often take several years from decision to construction, and consultation usually runs longer than the company's first schedule. I had assumed the partner comes first. That may be right, but the road could also be a condition the partner requires, so the order is an assumption until the filings show it. I have not checked the SEDAR+ filings, so this is a list of what to look for, not a finding.
