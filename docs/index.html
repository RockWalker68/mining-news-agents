Yukon Cornelius's answers

On Anglo's report, the prediction should count only from the first quarterly production report published after the one already logged. The log doesn't give Anglo's publication calendar, so I won't guess a date. Until a window is set, it can't be scored, and I'd drop the high confidence because we don't yet know the cause of the 5% fall.

The production report should show which of the three causes it is:

  • Milled tonnes flat but copper grade down points to grade.
  • Tonnes milled down with grade steady points to throughput, which could be maintenance or labour.
  • Recovery down with tonnes and grade steady points to metallurgy.
  • Operational commentary naming stoppages, strikes or planned shutdowns settles labour versus maintenance.

On treatment charges, the series I'd watch are the annual benchmark TC/RC settled between Chinese smelters and Antofagasta, and spot TC/RC quotes from the usual price reporters. The log gives me no figures, so I can't read them. I also need to correct myself: "concentrate tightens first" came from memory, not from a source. Treatment charges are a concentrate-market signal and often move before refined copper does, but a TC move tests concentrate balance, not the mine gap itself. I'd treat it as supporting evidence unless a prediction names the series, direction and date.

On the deep-sea comparison, a valid check needs both areas in square kilometres on the same basis: polygon areas computed from published coordinates, not headline "blocks." For the reserved area, I'd trust the International Seabed Authority's own published documents. For US claims, the primary source is NOAA's notices, including Federal Register filings. Mongabay and Bloomberg Law should be leads, not the figure source. Also note that US domestic claims and ISA reserved areas are different legal categories, so "rivaling" compares unlike things unless the desk explicitly says it's comparing them.

CREATIVE ANGLES:

(speculation level: high)

If it traces to maintenance or grade, Chile's policy change is largely a longer-horizon story. (speculation level: medium)

If US claims progress, deep-sea supply could eventually be a competitor to land-based concentrate, which would matter for the mine-gap thesis in the 2030s. Nothing in today's headlines supports a near-term effect. (speculation level: high)
