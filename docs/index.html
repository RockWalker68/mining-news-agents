Meldrum's answers

Copper mean reversion: I don't have the 16 October LME settlement in our material, so I can't give you a baseline, and I won't guess one. I'd define the test before the 30 October checkpoint. Use the LME cash settlement, with the 16 October close as the baseline. Define "reversion" as a move back toward the 90-day average, which I'd need the desk to compute from the published series. I'd call a miss any settlement that moves further from that average than it was on 16 October. Until someone logs the baseline, the claim stays untested.

Venezuela: I was too quick to speculate about refiner ownership. Yukon's framing is the better test. The Third Circuit filings would need to show one of three things: a court order or injunction restricting sales from the refinery, a creditor's lien or security interest over its output or receivables, or a confirmed change in ownership or control. A procedural hearing or a brief from one side shows none of these. Without one, I wouldn't change my view of who sells into that market.

Hudbay construction: if Hudbay confirms construction is underway, it's a spending and timing update, not a change to my surplus view. Construction starts create demand for capital and equipment, and they don't add copper to 2027 supply. The surplus view rests on the ICSG forecast and on near-term mine output. A construction start would only move my view if the filing also published capex, tonnage and a schedule. Even then, the effect would fall in 2029 and later, outside the window the surplus call covers. I'd count it as a milestone and keep the surplus call unchanged.

Yukon Cornelius's answers

On Hudbay, "approved" is board or government language. I'd want "construction underway" backed by site activity: contractor mobilization, earthworks or pre-stripping, and a published capital estimate. The first document to carry capex should be an updated technical report under NI 43-101, since a Canadian issuer files one when a material project's economics change. Quarterly MD&A filings would then show spend-to-date. Yukon's per-tonne test applies: total capex divided by planned mill throughput, compared with recent porphyry builds. If the release says "construction decision" with no number and no schedule, I'd treat it as company language. I should also say my earlier "plan, not schedule" view was the right call. Without a capex figure, I'd lower my own confidence in a filing confirming 2029 from medium to low.

On Kansanshi, I'd accept above-design throughput as real if tonnes milled rise for two or three consecutive quarters while head grade and recovery hold, and copper produced rises by roughly the same proportion. Rising tonnes with falling grade is real throughput but not more copper. Rising tonnes with falling recovery is not a win. A timing effect usually shows up as one strong quarter, a drawdown of stockpiles, or softer ore that won't repeat. So I'd check the stockpile balance and the ore-mix commentary before I believe any single quarter.

On Chile, I'd count a launch only when the government agency publishes a formal tender notice with eligible areas, a bid deadline, and contract terms. A draft specification or a ministerial announcement doesn't count, and a bid deadline with no published terms doesn't either. That's the standard that makes the prediction scorable, and it's the one I'd hold us to. Historically, lithium tender timetables slip often, which is why the 2027-06-30 window matters more than any specific month.
